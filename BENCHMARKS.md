## Honest takeaways

- The Rod-Joint Transform is built for one thing: point sequences whose consecutive edge vectors are related by a near-constant rotation+scale. `spiral.xy` is a pure logarithmic spiral (rotation+scale, no drift) and `helix.xyz` composes that same 2D joint on the (x,y) plane with a plain affine fit on z -- both are exactly what the joint models, which is why they beat general-purpose compressors by a wide margin (helix even beats lzma by ~4x). `toroidal.xyz` is the honest counterexample: its (x,y) magnitude itself oscillates (a wobbling radius, not a pure rotation), which a single calibrated rotation+scale constant -- even recalibrated every 128 rods -- cannot track, so lzma's dictionary matching wins there. That's a real, current limitation of the model, not a benchmark artifact.

- `random_walk.xy` is the deliberate adversarial case: consecutive rods have no consistent rotation/scale relationship, so the calibrated joint constant predicts poorly and CSA's advantage shrinks or disappears -- exactly what should happen, and worth showing rather than hiding.

- The Pantograph Lift (general mode) is a byte-stream transform; it competes with gzip/bz2/lzma on ordinary files but is not tuned to beat them on text -- its point is the geometric-mode story above, not replacing mature general compressors.

- GPU timing includes every host<->device transfer in the level-by-level loop. Whether it wins depends entirely on input size: small inputs are dominated by kernel-launch and PCIe overhead across ~log2(N) levels, exactly the same PCIe-bound pattern this repo owner's other GPU-resident projects (LiDAR pipeline, GPU-resident CKKS) already measured and documented -- see the table above for where the crossover actually falls on this machine, rather than assuming GPU always wins.
